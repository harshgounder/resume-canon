# Lane A: researchers and professors

Date of record: 2026-11-09. 13 people, 14 documents. Every one was opened and
text-extracted, so every entry below is V1: I read the document itself. No V2 or
V3 material was needed for the structural claims. Where a claim is about a
person's choice, the link in that subsection is the evidence.

Rule I applied: if the URL did not return a PDF, or an HTML page with real CV
content, the person does not get a row. Ten names from the brief failed that
test. They are listed under "What I could not verify" rather than padded with a
profile page or a lab bio.

least likely to publish a CV. They have lab pages, Google Scholar profiles, and
press bios. The document with dates, grants, and student placements is a
mid-career and junior habit.

## Terence Tao, mathematics

Source: [cv-long.html](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/cv-long.html) and
[cv-short.html](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/cv-short.html). HTML, not PDF.
The long version is about 11,000 words.

**Two documents, one person.** This is the only person in the sample who
publishes a short and a long CV side by side, with a nav bar reading "Biography
Short CV Full CV" on every page. The short one is not a summary of the long one;
it is the same sections with the headings marked "(selected)" and the lists
truncated.

**Order:** Biography, Research interests, Education, Appointments, Awards and
honors, Books, Publications, Teaching, Students, Postdoctoral mentees,
Professional service and editorial, Consulting activities, Selected lectures,
Patents.

**Contact block:** email and homepage only, plus the title line "Professor of
Mathematics; James and Carol Collins Chair, University of California, Los
Angeles". No phone, no postal address, no photo.

**Bullet grammar:** noun phrases with a leading year. "1996 Ph.D., Princeton
University (advisor: Elias Stein)". Awards are "year, prize, awarding body",
for example "2006 Fields Medal, International Mathematical Union". No verbs.

**Education** sits third, after the bio and the research interests. **Skills:**
none. **Publications:** no numbering at all, grouped into Books and Publications,
one line each with venue and year.

The short CV's honesty is the interesting part. It says "Awards and honors
(selected)" in the heading instead of silently dropping half the list.

## Scott Aaronson, quantum complexity

Source: [vita.pdf](https://www.scottaaronson.com/vita.pdf), 12 pages, dated May
10, 2026.

**Order:** Education, Fellowships and Other Positions, Faculty Positions, Awards,
Grants and Fellowships, Research Papers, Books, Expository Writing and Reviews,
Teaching, Students and Postdocs, Professional Service.

**Contact block:** six centred lines: chair title, centre directorship,
department, university, city, email, homepage, then the date. No phone, no
street address.

**Bullet grammar:** citation strings, not achievement statements. Every paper is
"S. Aaronson. Title, Venue, pp. 168-177, 1997." The only verb-led lines are in
Faculty Positions, where promotions are recorded: "Began as Assistant Professor
in Fall 2007; promoted to Associate Professor with Tenure in Spring 2013."

**Education** is first, above the fold. **Skills:** none. **Publications:** a
single chronological list with bullets, no numbers, no links, no bold titles.
Submitted papers sit at the end with arXiv IDs and the word "submitted".

**Grants:** the section exists but lists only fellowships and career awards, no
dollar amounts. **Talks:** there is no talks section at all, which is rare here.

## Percy Liang, NLP and ML

Source: [cv.pdf](https://cs.stanford.edu/~pliang/cv.pdf), 16 pages. No printed
date; the file was generated in May 2024.

**Order:** Personal Details, Education, Experience, Awards and Honors,
Publications, Professional Activities, Teaching.

**Contact block:** two lines. "Name: Percy Liang" and "Email:
pliang@cs.stanford.edu". That is the whole block. No address, no phone, no
homepage.

**Bullet grammar:** year first, then a noun phrase, in a two-column table
layout: "2019 Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers
(PECASE)". In Experience, internships from 2002 are still listed: "2002 Intern
at Google, Mountain View, CA". Nothing is trimmed for seniority.

**Education** is second. **Skills:** none. **Publications** are grouped into Book
chapters, Refereed journal publications, Peer-reviewed conference papers, and
each group restarts numbering at 1. So there is no single "paper 214" handle,
unlike Griffiths and Jurafsky. No links, no DOIs.

**Service** is one dense paragraph with bold run-in labels ("Reviewer for
conferences", "Area chair for conferences") rather than a bulleted list.
**Teaching** lists course numbers and years, including two Berkeley TA roles
from 2007 and 2008.

## Thomas L. Griffiths, cognitive science

Source: [cv.pdf](https://cocosci.princeton.edu/tom/cv.pdf), 44 pages. The
longest document in the sample. No printed date.

**Order:** Personal Details, Professional Positions, Education, Awards and
Honors, Grants and Funding, Publication List, Invited Talks, Other Talks and
Conference Presentations, Professional Activities, Lab Alumni.

**Contact block:** email, physical mail as a five-line address block, and
nationality ("Citizen of Australia, the United Kingdom, & the United States of
America"). No phone. Page number sits top right, section headings are all caps.

**How 44 pages stays usable:** all-caps section headers, lettered subsections
("External", "Internal" under grants; "Books", "Journal articles",
"Peer-reviewed conference papers", "Book chapters" under publications), and one
continuous publication number across every category, running to 519. A reader
can cite "publication 214" and it means one thing.

**Grants** carry dates, role, funder, and amount: "2025-2027 "Predicting
algorithmic trust at scale," (with 6 other faculty members, Thomas Griffiths as
PI), DARPA ($4,997,132)." **Publications** open with a metric: "84,000+
citations, h index of 126 via Google Scholar".

**Education** is third, after positions. **Skills:** none. **Bullets:** noun
phrases, no verbs. The CV ends with Lab Alumni and each student's placement
("Sharon Goldwater (Reader, University of Edinburgh)"), which is a placement
table disguised as a list.

## Dan Jurafsky, linguistics and NLP

Source: [cv.pdf](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/cv.pdf), 19 pages. No printed
date.

**Order:** contact block, Education, Academic Employment, Honors Awards and
Fellowships, Thesis awards for student advisees, Research (books, journals, book
chapters, recent conference papers), Selected Service, Student and Postdoc
Advisees.

**Contact block:** two department names, university, postal address, email,
homepage. No phone. Footer is name plus page number.

**The unusual section** is "Thesis awards for student advisees", which sits
directly under his own honors: "Arthur Samuel Doctoral Thesis Award for the top
Stanford CS PhD thesis: Will Hamilton, 2019". He puts his students' prizes at the
same level as his own.

**Bullet grammar:** numbered citations, continuous across every category, 1 to
303. "1. Jurafsky, Dan. 2014. The Language of Food. W. W. Norton." No links.
Conference papers are headed "Recent Refereed Conference Papers", so the section
name admits the list is partial.

**Education** is second, before employment. **Skills:** none. **Advising** is a
long reverse-chronological list with degree and year for each student, going back
to 2001.

## Fei-Fei Li, computer vision

Source: [LiFei-Fei_ProfessionalCV_noref.pdf](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/feifeili/LiFei-Fei_ProfessionalCV_noref.pdf),

## Andreas Krause, ML and optimization

Source: [krause-cv.pdf](https://las.inf.ethz.ch/files/krause-cv.pdf), 24 pages,
dated November 2017.

**Order:** Contact Information, Educational Background, Positions Held, Visiting
Positions, Academic Honors and Recognitions, Teaching Awards, Professional
Activities (summer school, workshop, editorship, reviewing), Courses Taught,
Advising and Thesis Committees, Invited Talks and Seminars, Publications
(journals, selective conferences, magazine articles, book chapters, thesis).

**Contact block:** two-column, address left, voice, fax, web, email right. The
headings are LaTeX small caps, which is why text extraction renders them as
"C ONTACT I NFORMATION".

**The distinctive habit:** conference entries end with the acceptance rate.
"Acceptance rate: 25%" appears after individual papers. That is a claim about
selectivity attached to each line, and it makes the list read as evidence rather
than inventory.

**Education** is second. **Skills:** none. **Grants:** there is no grants section
at all, which is odd for an ETH professor with industry funding. **Bullets:**
mixed, some numbered citations, some bulleted items.

## Jason Wei, LLM research

Source: [Jason_Wei_CV.pdf](https://www.jasonwei.net/s/Jason_Wei___CV-f3xl.pdf),
2 pages. No printed date.

**Order:** Employment, Notable Publications, Invited Talks, Blog Posts, Videos.

That is the whole CV. There is no education section. There is no skills section,
no projects, no service, no teaching. Two pages, and half of page two is blog
posts and videos.

**Contact block:** name, gmail address, homepage. Three lines.

**Bullet grammar:** none. Each entry is a date in a left column and a sentence on
the right. Publications are deliberately selected, eight of them, and the section
is called "Notable Publications". Authors are compressed hard: "{J. Wei, Z.
Sun}, and eight others." and "A. Chowdhery, S. Narang, J. Devlin, and 64 others
including J. Wei."

**Why it works:** the reader learns in ten seconds what he is known for. The
chain-of-thought and emergent-abilities papers are on the first page. The cost is
that a recruiter looking for a degree finds nothing.

## What repeats

Counts are over the 13 people. Where Tao has two files I counted the long one for
structure.

| Pattern | Count | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Contact block at the top with an email address | 13 of 13 | universal |
| No skills section | 12 of 13 | only the PhD candidate has one |
| No photograph | 13 of 13 | `pdfimages` found zero images in all 12 PDFs |
| No "references available on request" | 13 of 13 | the line does not exist in this population |
| Education in the first three sections | 12 of 13 | only Jason Wei omits education entirely |
| Bullets are noun phrases, never "Led" or "Built" | 13 of 13 | no past-tense achievement verbs anywhere |
| A service or professional activities section near the end | 11 of 13 | absent in Wei and Jiang |
| An advising or students section | 9 of 13 | usually with placement of each student |
| Publications grouped by type (book, journal, conference) | 7 of 13 | the rest use one chronological list |
| Publications numbered | 8 of 13 | two schemes: continuous across groups, or restart per group |
| A talks section | 8 of 13 | absent in Aaronson, Liang, Jurafsky, Dao, Jiang |
| Page numbers on every page | 9 of 13 | absent in Li, Gonzalez, Jiang; Tao is HTML |
| A summary or bio paragraph at the top | 3 of 13 | Tao, Gonzalez, Lambert |
| A funding or grants section | 5 of 13 | dollar amounts in only 2 (Griffiths, Li) |
| Visible URLs in the text layer | 0 to 3 per document | links are hyperlinks, not printed URLs |

Two schemes for numbering show up about equally. Griffiths and Jurafsky run one
counter across books, journals, and conferences, so item 517 exists and can be
cited. Liang restarts at 1 inside each group. Lambert numbers continuously but
separates by year. The continuous scheme is better for citation; the per-group
scheme is better for reading.

Four publication formats, all defensible:

- Citation string, author first, no bold: Aaronson.
- Title first, authors second, venue third: Lambert.
- ID plus full citation plus a [pdf] link: Min.
- Plain paragraph, no list marker: Dao.

The one thing nobody does is bold the paper title and put the venue in grey.
These are LaTeX documents built for print, not web pages.

## What surprised me

**The most famous people do not publish CVs.** Ten of the fifteen names in the
brief had nothing openable. The ones who do publish are mid-career, or junior and
on the market. Fame substitutes for the document.

**A two-page CV with no education section exists at OpenAI.** Jason Wei's file is
the shortest by a factor of three and it omits the section every guide says must
be first. It works because the publications are the credential.

**Public CVs rot quietly.** Fei-Fei Li's is dated 2011 and still sits on a
Stanford URL. Krause's is dated 2017. Both are the top result for "CV" on the
person's own site. A CV is a snapshot, not a maintained record, and the link
survives long after the content stops.

**Tao publishes two lengths on purpose.** Not a summary and a full version, but
the same document with "(selected)" in the headings. That is a more honest way to
shorten a CV than deleting entries and pretending the list is complete.

**One continuous publication number beats a table of contents.** Griffiths has 44
pages, no TOC, and 519 numbered items. The number is the navigation.

**Acceptance rates as a per-paper annotation.** Krause prints "Acceptance rate:
25%" next to conference papers. Nobody else does this. It converts a list of
titles into a claim about selectivity.

**An "impact" section above the publications.** Dao's "Research Adoption" lists
frameworks and companies using his code, before the paper list. That is a
different theory of what a CV is for.

**A 33-page CV with no page numbers.** Gonzalez's document is the longest PDF and
the only long one with no pagination. Length and care do not scale together.

**Skills sections belong to juniors.** One of thirteen, and it is the PhD
candidate. The senior documents replace skills with evidence: courses taught,
software released, students placed.
