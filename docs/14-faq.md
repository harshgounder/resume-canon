# 14. FAQ

Short answers, with the reason and the exception.

**One page or two?**
One page for students and anyone under about eight years. Two when the content
earns it. Google says it has no length requirement; Yale says one page for
students; Imperial says one to two. The split follows the market, not the
evidence.

**Do I need a photo?**
US, Canada, UK, Australia: no. India: only if the portal requires it, and never
in the document when the channel says not to. Japan, Korea: yes. Germany: often.
Follow the channel, not the country stereotype.

**Objective or summary?**
Neither, usually. Google and AWS say drop it. A summary earns its space only if
it is specific: what you are, what you are unusually good at, what you want next.
An objective statement is about you, which is the wrong subject.

**Should I list my GPA?**
If it is above the local cutoff for the roles you want, or you are a student with
no experience. Drop it after the first job. Never convert CGPA to percentage
with a formula the employer has not published.

**What about hobbies?**
Only if they are evidence: national level sport, open source maintainership, a
published thing. Hobbies also leak demographic signals to LLM screeners, which is
a reason to keep them minimal.

**References available on request?**
Delete the line. Recruiters assume references exist and ask later.

**How long should a bullet be?**
One to two lines on the page. Word counts are vendor consensus, not measurement.
If it wraps to three lines, split or cut it.

**How many bullets per role?**
Two to five. One or two for old roles.

**Should I use "I"?**
No. Start with the verb.

**Can I use AI to write it?**
Use it to critique and to compress, not to invent. The failure mode is
generic prose and fabricated metrics, and both are detectable in an interview.
Keep a master resume and make any tool work only from it.

**Does an ATS score matter?**
No ATS vendor sells one. Third party scores disagree by 20 or more points on the
same document. Use them as a keyword checklist.

**Why am I getting no replies?**
In order of likelihood: eligibility or knockout answers, a parse failure, a
mismatch between the title and the requisition, applying late, or volume. The
document is the fourth or fifth suspect, not the first.

**Do cover letters matter?**
Sometimes, and mostly as a writing sample. If optional, skip it unless you have
something specific to say that the resume cannot carry.

**Should I tailor every application?**
Tailor the top third and the keyword set. Rewriting every bullet for every
application is a full time job that does not pay.

**Two column layout, yes or no?**
No, unless you have run the parse test and the extracted text reads in order. The
risk is silent and the upside is aesthetic.

**Can I put a photo in the PDF if the form also asks for one?**
If the form asks, the form has it. Do not duplicate it in the document unless the
document is the only channel.

**What file name?**
`Firstname_Lastname_Role.pdf`. No spaces, no "final", no version numbers.

**What about a portfolio link?**
Yes, if it resolves. A dead link is worse than no link.

**How many pages for an academic CV?**
No limit, but the first two pages must stand alone. Put the strongest evidence
there and number the pages.

**Is LaTeX better than Word?**
Only for the output. LaTeX gives a clean text layer and stable typography. Word
is fine if you avoid text boxes, tables for layout, and inconsistent styles.

**What if I have no projects and no internships?**
Use coursework with a real output (a compiler, a database, a game), open source
contributions, or a freelance task. A tutorial clone does not count. Build
something small that someone else uses, then write it up.

**How do I explain a gap?**
Date it honestly. One line in the summary if it needs explaining. Do not switch
to a functional resume.

**Do I need a GitHub?**
For engineering roles, yes, and reviewers click it. A repo with a README, tests,
and more than one commit is the difference between a claim and evidence.

**Should I include every technology I know?**
No. List what you can defend for ten minutes. Every listed skill is an invitation
to be tested.
