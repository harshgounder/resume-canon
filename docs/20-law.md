# 20. Law, privacy and bias

What employers may ask, what you can demand, and what the evidence says about
who gets filtered.

> Evidence: wave 08 lane M (law, privacy, bias, 6,846 words, 57 sources).
> [research/wave-08/law-privacy-bias.md](../research/wave-08/law-privacy-bias.md)

## The short version

- No regulator is coming to save your application. The strongest law in the US
  is barely enforced, the federal guidance is gone, and India's data law has no
  automated decision right.
- Bias in screening is real, and it is concentrated in a minority of firms.
- The levers that work for a candidate are small and specific: refuse salary
  history where banned, request erasure, and remove photo, date of birth and
  address where they are optional.
- The folklore that does not work: "school name gets you filtered" and "GDPR
  explains any AI rejection".

## The rules that exist

| Jurisdiction | Instrument | What it requires | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU | AI Act, Annex III point 4 | Employment and worker management systems are high risk: risk management, logging, human oversight, transparency | Obligations apply 2 December 2027 after the 2026 Omnibus deferral |
| EU | AI Act, Article 5(1)(f) | Bans emotion recognition in the workplace | Already in force since 2 February 2025, which catches enthusiasm-scoring video tools now |
| New York City | Local Law 144 | Annual bias audit for automated employment decision tools, published summary | In force, enforcement hollow |
| Colorado | SB 26-189, replacing SB 24-205 | Disclosure, human review, data correction, AG-only enforcement | SB 24-205 never took effect. The replacement was signed 14 May 2026, effective 1 January 2027 |
| US federal | EEOC AI guidance | Technical assistance on AI and hiring | Removed 27 January 2025. The 2026 enforcement plan never mentions AI, and the 2012 criminal history guidance was rescinded 29 October 2026 |
| India | DPDP Act 2023 | Consent, access, correction and erasure, grievance, nomination | Rules notified November 2025, phased to mid-May 2027. No automated decision right exists |
| UK | Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 | Automated decision provisions | Commencement expected late 2025, not verified |

## NYC Local Law 144, in practice

It is the only US law that mandates a bias audit by name, and the enforcement
numbers are the story:

- The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection received two complaints in two
  years.
- It reviewed 32 companies and found one issue.
- State auditors found at least 17 potential violations.
- 75 percent of 311 test calls were misrouted (Comptroller report 2024-N-6,
  December 2025).
- The audit has no pass threshold, so passing it means publishing a number, not
  being fair.

## What India's law does not give you

The DPDP Act 2023 gives rights of access, correction and erasure, grievance
redress, and nomination (sections 11 to 14). It does not give a right to an
explanation of an automated decision, and it does not give a right to human
review of one.

Any guide telling Indian candidates to demand an AI explanation is describing a
right that does not exist in Indian law. What does exist: the right to ask what
data is held, to have it corrected, and to have it erased, with a 90-day response
window and penalties up to 250 crore rupees.

## Bias, measured

The best recent evidence is firm-level, not industry-level:

- Across 83,000 applications to 108 large US employers, Black-sounding names
  carried a 2.1 percentage point contact penalty.
- The penalty was significant at 23 firms, and the top quintile of firms
  accounted for about half of all lost contacts.
- School prestige was null in a 2,400 application test across the US, UK and
  Australia, while skills match produced a 79 percent improvement.
- Polish did not remove the name penalty across 13,162 responses in five
  countries.

Two implications, and they point in different directions:

1. If you are being filtered, the firm may matter more than your document.
2. Presentation cannot fix a name penalty, and pretending otherwise is cruel.

## What you can actually do

| Lever | Where it works | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Refuse salary history | US states and cities with bans | The question is often unlawful, and answering it anchors you low |
| Request erasure of your data | EU, UK, India | Removes you from the pipeline and from future searches |
| Remove photo, DOB, address | US, UK, Canada, Australia, and India where optional | They carry bias signals and add parse risk |
| Apply to firms with published audits | NYC, and any employer that publishes | The audit is weak, but publication correlates with process |
| Use the referral path | Everywhere | It bypasses the filter that carries the bias |

## What does not work

- **"GDPR means they cannot reject me by algorithm."** It means they must tell
  you certain things and let you object in some cases. It does not stop the
  rejection.
- **"A prestigious school guarantees the screen."** Null in the largest test in
  the sample.
- **"A polished document removes bias."** The name penalty survived polish in
  five countries.
- **"Asking for the algorithm's explanation."** No such right in the US or India,
  and a limited one in the EU that arrives years after the decision.

## The honest position

The document is one variable in a system with law, firm behaviour, volume and
bias in it. This repo can make the document as good as the evidence allows. It
cannot make the system fair, and it should not pretend that craft is a substitute
for law.
